On April 14, 2026, a Parent Teacher Committee (PTC) meeting was held at BES with no more than 100 attendees starting at 7:00 pm and lasting for roughly 1.5 hours. In this meeting, Dr. Smith spent the majority of the time providing her own assessment of the situtation with a PowerPoint slide deck for support.

Below are summary points that Dr. Smith focused on that will be relevant for this analysis.
- Pictures of the classes with statements that children were not "on top of each other" (I am assuming this particular statement may have been in response to specific feedback provided by families).
- Discussion about "cubbies" and use of an "open classroom" as overflow.
- Reiterating the points of the previous email using Enrollment data supporting the idea that:
-- This class size event was a fluke.
-- These are typical class sizes in this area.
-- We cannot project

The following Q and A are paraphrasing of the interactions with Dr. Smith (unless quoted) at the conclusion of her presentation. The audio recording can be found here, along with timestamps of the individual questions.

### 0:07:50 - Population Projections
**Question:**
How are population projections used to support enrollment planning?

**Response:**
Population projections are used however the enrollment numbers are the primary indicator. It was not well answered as to how population projections impact overall headcount decisions in UMASD.

### 0:09:45 - Loss of Classroom Teachers across UMASD Elementary schools
**Question:**
In the slides, all but one Elementary school lost a classroom teacher on the same year, with Candlebrook eventually losing one as well. Where did that resource go?

**Response:**
The headcount was redeployed or retired with no replacement, which means negative headcount over time.

### 0:17:10 - Classroom Teacher level
**Question:**
If you maintained the number of classroom teachers, wouldn't the class size not be an issue?

**Response:**
We would not have had an issue if the two teachers were assigned to third grade and the three teachers to second grade.

### 0:18:25 - Enrollment trends and classroom teachers
**Question:**
If there is not a trend showing lower enrollment, why are we cutting number of teachers?

**Response:**
"The numbers do not support having an extra teacher."

### 0:20:07 - Highlights variation of enrollment
**Question:**
If you had maintained the number of classroom teachers, wouldn't this issue had been avoided? The example of a teacher retiring from Roberts Elementary was also cited as a similar example of where lost headcount was not backfilled.

**Response:**
The issue is the wrong class was selected for fewer classroom teachers. A decision was made for 2nd grade to have the fewest teachers.

### 0:21:53 - Preventing future allotment issues
**Question:**
If allotment of teachers by grade is the issue, how will you prevent that in the future?

**Response:**
"We are not going to plan for anomalies." What are we going to do now? We have a new principal. I am not blaming the previous principal because I approved it, but also the principal knows the school best. The process is not going to change.

### 0:23:48 - Variance of Student Enrollment
**Question:**
There is a mean of 268.5 students with a standard deviation of 10, which is a %RSD of 3.7% which means there is no significant change in enrollment. The principal is in charge of allotment of teachers but not overall headcount in the district correct?

**Response:**
Prinipal does in consultation with HR. Prinipals have come to Superintended asking for resources and some things we can get and some we can't. Dr. Smith stated that they would have to justify adding, where audience member stated it was not adding, it was maintaining headcount.
"We can do a lot of different formulas, we can do a lot of different scenarios, I'm just telling you that this is the process that we all use, and I am still going to say it, again (repeats point about selection of second grade for one fewer classroom teacher)
